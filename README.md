### 🔗 [View the live RYH website](https://dzaagrebiina.github.io/RYH/)

The idea in a nutshell

Most wearables are built for adults chasing performance numbers. Teenagers get handed the same dashboards, and parents are left choosing between knowing nothing and checking up on their child's every move.

RYH tries to sit in the middle:
For teens (10–18): a discreet ring and an app that explains what the data actually means in plain language, instead of throwing charts at them.
For parents (30–45): a calmer view of wellbeing trends, built around consent and conversation rather than tracking.

Market: planned as a direct-to-consumer launch in the UK.

Why a landing page?

The site wasn't just a shop window. It was one of my research tools. I used it to see how real people react to the concept before writing the marketing plan:
Sign-up form to gauge genuine interest.
Microsoft Clarity to watch how visitors actually behave on the page: where they click, how far they scroll, and where they lose interest.
Pricing section to test how people respond to the proposed price points.
What visitors did on the page fed directly into the report, alongside what they told me in surveys and interviews.

The research behind it

The report combines three kinds of primary research with secondary data:
Survey of the target audience covering wearable habits, privacy concerns and willingness to pay.
Interviews used to stress-test the personas I had built from secondary research.
Behavioural analytics from Microsoft Clarity on this live site (session behaviour, scroll depth and click distribution).
Market sizing draws on ONS population data, YouGov and IDC. The analysis uses frameworks such as TAM–SAM–SOM, STP, the 4Ps and Jobs-to-be-Done, and checks the plan against UK GDPR, the ICO Children's Code and the CAP Code, since the product is aimed at under-18s.
All of this is written up in full in the two PDFs above.

How it was built?

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript in a single file, no frameworks and no build step.
Hosted on GitHub Pages, so every change committed to main goes live within a minute or two.
Mobile-responsive, because the audience is mostly going to see it on a phone.
AI-assisted: I used AI tools to help draft the code, then directed the structure, copy, branding and changes myself. The same approach is described in the report.

Brand

Warm oat backgrounds, a forest green primary colour, terracotta for the parents' side and sage for the teens' side. The aim was something that feels calm and trustworthy rather than "techy".

A note on privacy

This site uses Microsoft Clarity to record anonymous usage data (clicks, scrolling and session replays) for research purposes. Clarity's default masking settings are in place, which hide what people type into form fields. No location data is collected.
If you've visited the site and have any questions about this, feel free to get in touch through GitHub.
